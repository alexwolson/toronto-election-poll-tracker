# 01: Turn generic council signals into race-specific directional explanations

**What to build:** Replace the generic statistical prose beneath council candidates and incumbents with concise explanations of what each fired signal actually means for that person and race. Candidate-history signals should pair a short, specific fact with an upward or downward zigzag/trend icon indicating a historically positive or negative direction without implying a probability or effect size. Incumbent exposure explanations should translate the three Councillor Defeatability Index components and any separately fired triggers into concrete ward facts, combining and deduplicating related information. Carry the facts and provenance needed to produce those explanations through the council-card feed instead of publishing only static catalog copy; preserve the existing statistical definitions, gates, and non-predictive framing.

**Blocked by:** None (can start immediately).

**Status:** ready-for-agent

- [ ] Every displayed candidate-history signal names the relevant candidate or opponent and states the actual fact that caused the signal to appear; none repeats the current generic catalog sentences about “qualifying elected-office,” “Returning councillors,” or historical candidates in the abstract.
- [ ] Each displayed candidate-history signal has an upward or downward zigzag/trend icon for historically positive or negative direction. The icon does not imply magnitude, probability, causality, or a forecast, and has an accessible text equivalent such as “positive historical signal” or “negative historical signal.”
- [ ] Direction is derived from the candidate's actual value and the measured association, not merely from the association's coefficient. The decision rules are documented and deterministically tested for all six supported hint types, including missing values, zero victories, positive and negative prior margins, own-history signals, and opponent-history signals.
- [ ] Naser Kaid's Ward 20 explanations correctly treat his two trustee losses as qualifying non-council history: his most recent qualifying result was a seventh-place finish in 2022, roughly 26 percentage points behind the winner, and his observed elected-office victory count is zero. Both are presented as negative signals; the page never shows positive-sounding boilerplate about “stronger results” or “each additional victory” without saying where Kaid actually falls.
- [ ] Malik Ahmad's three unsuccessful council races do not become qualifying own-history signals under the existing methodology, because unsuccessful council history is included only for a Returning councillor. The presentation makes no unsupported claim about Ahmad and does not require readers to understand that internal vocabulary.
- [ ] Candidate history distinguishes a measured zero from missing coverage. Kaid's zero victories may produce a specific negative explanation because he has confirmed qualifying candidacies; a candidate with no resolved qualifying history does not receive the same claim.
- [ ] Opponent-history information is not arbitrarily gated by whether the subject candidate has linked history. A ward-wide opponent fact must not appear beneath only the identity-matched subset of candidates who face that opponent.
- [ ] Opponent-history signals that merely duplicate first-class incumbent information are either omitted from individual candidate cards or surfaced once in an appropriate race-level location. If an opponent signal adds distinct information and is shown, it identifies the opponent and the specific prior result behind it. In Ward 20, the UI does not attach the same unexplained Parthi Kandavel-derived value to Ahmad, Kaid, Lisciandro, and Rupasinghe while silently omitting it for Laikul Choudhury solely because Choudhury has no linked history.
- [ ] Explanations are deduplicated against the visible past-races list and incumbent summary. A signal may concisely interpret those facts, but it does not restate them in longer or more technical language.
- [ ] The feed retains enough structured provenance to explain every published candidate signal, including the subject, source candidate where applicable, source race, office type, date, result, margin or victory count, and coverage state. If the facts required for an honest explanation cannot be resolved, omit the explanation instead of falling back to generic prose.
- [ ] Incumbent exposure explanations use the ward's actual values. For Ward 11, the growth explanation reads equivalently to: “University–Rosedale has an estimated 8,869 more voters than in 2022—far more than Dianne Saxe's 123-vote winning margin.”
- [ ] A high Councillor Defeatability Index result is explained through what its three measurements mean for this race: prior vote share, the incumbent's votes as a share of eligible electors, and electorate growth relative to the winning margin. Where a separate trigger already explains one component, the index explanation covers the remaining meaning instead of repeating it.
- [ ] Ward 11's combined presentation reads equivalently to the approved growth sentence plus: “Dianne Saxe won with 35% of votes cast and support from 11% of eligible voters—both among the lowest for Toronto incumbents.” It does not use unexplained phrases such as “combined index” or “structurally exposed.”
- [ ] City Hall Watcher and the Councillor Defeatability Index remain properly attributed. A concise fallback such as “Dianne Saxe has received a high Councillor Defeatability Index rating from City Hall Watcher” is used only when a more meaningful component-based explanation genuinely cannot be produced, not when the component facts are available.
- [ ] Multiple candidate hints and incumbent triggers are composed into a short, scannable group with a brief “historical context, not a forecast” explanation. Existing trigger thresholds, study associations, evidence gates, and council attention logic remain unchanged.
- [ ] The historical-hint display contract and related decision documentation are updated wherever they currently require exact static `frontend_copy`, so the new structured, candidate-specific presentation cannot silently drift back to generic prose.
- [ ] End-to-end regression coverage includes Ward 20's Ahmad, Kaid, Kandavel, and Choudhury cases; Ward 11's growth and high-index combination; every supported candidate-history hint type; every incumbent exposure-trigger type; missing provenance; accessible icon labels; and the absence of the retired generic phrases in the rendered council pages.
